Per-layer group scheduling: a node-level profile of `--mode loop --group-entry`
=================================================================================

    cd C:\fhwt
    nsys profile --force-overwrite true --cuda-event-trace=false \
        --cuda-graph-trace=node -o g_w6 \
        -- .\build\fhwt.exe --mode loop --rows 512 --dims 128 --layers 8 \
           --tensors 6 --group-entry --streams 6 --iters 20 --reps 3

The report is this directory's loop_group_width.nsys-rep; this file is the export.
RTX 5060 Ti / sm_120 / 36 SM / L2 32 MiB / CUDA 12.9 / Windows.  Workload: 8 layers
x 6 tensors = 48 [512,128] fp16 rotations per pass, each layer's 6 tensors sharing
one anchor -- strategy D built with --group-entry (report 7.13).

The run covers strategy C and strategy D: 1 correctness pass + 2 warm-up passes
+ 3 reps x 20 timed passes each.  A CUDA Graph is one opaque record at the default
granularity, so --cuda-graph-trace=node is what makes a node visible at all; with it
every graph node is its own kernel record and the records of one replay share a
correlation id, which is what the split below uses.

1. The kernels
--------------
Kernel records: 15696, of which 15120 are the single kernel
`fhwt_reg_kernel<__half, Native2<__half>, 7, 5>`, all with the same launch geometry
128x128, median 1376 ns (min 832 / p90 1664 / max 517131).  C and D therefore
execute an identical node list and everything below is scheduling.
The other 576 records are the correctness probe's device-to-host compares, the
memsets between phases and the buffer fills.

2. Chain (C) vs per-layer fork/join (D)
---------------------------------------
A replay is a correlation id carrying all 48 nodes.  There are 126 of them:
1 correctness + 2 warm-up + 60 timed each for C and D.  Per replay, span = last-node
end - first-node start, busy = sum of node durations, concurrency = peak number of
nodes in flight at once (a sweep over start/end events).

| strategy | replays | span p50 (us) | span p10 | span p90 | busy p50 (us) | span/busy | peak concurrency |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C, 48-node chain | 60 | 58.0 | 57.9 | 58.1 | 47.0 | 1.23 | 1 |
| D, 8 x (1+5) fork/join | 60 | 25.0 | 24.9 | 25.1 | 54.3 | 0.46 | 5 |

The chain is one node wide by construction: span (58.0 us) *exceeds* busy (47.0 us)
by 10.9 us -- a ~232 ns bubble between consecutive nodes -- and peak concurrency is 1.
Each rotation only fills a fraction of the 36 SMs, yet the pass costs the sum of
their durations.

The forked graph inverts it: span 25.0 us < busy 54.3 us, span/busy 0.46, peak
concurrency 5.  With 6 tensors per layer one node is the anchor and the other 5 run
together, so the pass pays 8 serialisation points instead of 48.  Per transform:
C 1.207 us (= 58.0 / 48), D 0.520 us (= 25.0 / 48).  D's 0.52 us is *below* the
1.376 us median duration of the kernel itself, which is only possible because the
siblings really do overlap.  Both reproduce the event-timed figures in
data/group.csv (1.18 us for C, 0.60 us for D) from a second, independent
measurement -- which is the point of keeping a profile as well as a stopwatch.

3. Host side: cudaGraphLaunch, and what a node costs
----------------------------------------------------
| graph | launches | p50 (us) | p90 (us) | max (us) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| single node (strategy B) | 3024 | 5.2 | 8.4 | 136.8 |
| C, 48-node chain | 60 | 50.9 | 67.0 | 155.7 |
| D, 48-node fork/join | 60 | 39.0 | 52.3 | 91.8 |

All three have a single root (layer 0's anchor is queued with add()), so none of
them pays the multi-root tax of 5.8 / 8.6 -- 1 root submits at ~12 us there, 8 roots
at 35-58 us.  The 7.13 win therefore comes from the DAG shape, not the submit path.

What this capture does add is a second, independent warning: the submit cost grows
with the node count.  A 1-node graph costs 5.2 us at p50 and a 48-node one 39.0 us,
a difference of ~34 us for the 47 extra nodes (~0.7 us/node, which fits 8.6's
8-node single-root graph at 11.7-12.0 us).  Read at face value, D's host cost exceeds
its own GPU span (25.0 us) -- but D's measured pass time is 0.602 us per transform,
i.e. 28.9 us per pass, *below* that.  So these submit figures are inflated by the
profiler and the desktop load; the inflation-robust reading is the difference, ~34
us for 47 extra nodes.  At 48 nodes the submit path is the same order of magnitude as
the work, which is why the next lever here is to put *more passes* into one graph,
not to reshape the graph again.

4. What it says
---------------
1. Independence is only worth anything if it is expressed as *width*: a chain of 48
   nodes and a per-layer fork/join of the same 48 nodes differ by 2.3x at the GPU,
   on identical kernels.
2. Width has to sit *inside* a layer.  Making all 48 nodes independent (the flat
   fan-out of 7.11) does not help; at 48 nodes it was the worst of the shapes, and
   the reason is visible here -- the only useful overlap is between the 5 siblings of
   one layer, and nothing else.
3. The remaining distance to ideal (48 nodes of busy packed into 25.0 us rather than
   54.3 us, span/busy 0.46) is DRAM traffic, not scheduling: at [512,128] a pass
   touches 2 MiB and the 5 siblings contend for the same L2/DRAM, so they overlap
   only partially.  That is why the win disappears at rows = 16384 (report 7.13).
